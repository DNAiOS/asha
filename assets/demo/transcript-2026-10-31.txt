**Labetalol, nifedipine (extended-release), and methyldopa are the standard first-line choices in pregnancy. ACE inhibitors and ARBs are contraindicated.** 

Reviews place methyldopa, beta-blockers (labetalol most often) and calcium channel blockers as first- or second-line, with ACE inhibitors, ARBs and most diuretics avoided because the risk outweighs the benefit . For acute severe hypertension, IV labetalol or oral nifedipine are reasonable, and hydralazine is also used . The Canadian consensus treats systolic above 169 or diastolic above 109 mm Hg as an emergency needing prompt drug treatment . Treatment thresholds elsewhere are 140/90 mm Hg in defined higher-risk groups . Mild-to-moderate hypertension may not clearly reduce maternal or fetal risk with treatment . Thiazides are a second-line option, and data on ARBs are sparse, with effects assumed similar to ACE inhibitors .

**Any patient on an ACE inhibitor or ARB who becomes pregnant or plans to should be switched under supervision, not stopped abruptly without a replacement plan.**

Evidence caveats: two retrieved papers are retracted and were not used . A preprint reports differing placental vascular effects between agents, which is preliminary only . Choice and dose depend on gestational age, severity, and comorbidities, which remain your call.
